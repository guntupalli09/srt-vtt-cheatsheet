SRT vs VTT at a glance

SRT (SubRip) has no formal standard; it is defined by long-standing convention. WebVTT is specified by the W3C in WebVTT: The Web Video Text Tracks Format. The WebVTT rows below follow that document.

Structural differences between SRT and WebVTT
FeatureSRTWebVTT (.vtt)
File headerNone. The file starts with the first cue number.Required. The first line is WEBVTT, optionally followed by a space or tab and other text, then a blank line. An optional byte order mark may come before it.
Cue numberingA number above every cue, by convention 1, 2, 3 and so on.Optional cue identifier on the line above the timing. Any text without -->; must be unique in the file.
Timestamp00:01:02,500: hours, minutes, seconds, then a comma and three digits of milliseconds.00:01:02.500: a period and exactly three digits. Hours are optional when zero (01:02.500).
Timing linestart --> endstart --> end, with one or more spaces or tabs around the arrow. The end must be after the start.
Cue settingsNot part of the convention. Some players read position codes, many ignore them.Optional after the end time: vertical, line, position, size, align, region.
CommentsNone.NOTE blocks, ignored by players.
StylingInformal tags many players accept, such as <i>, <b>, <u> and <font color>. Support varies.Defined tags: <c>, <i>, <b>, <u>, <ruby>/<rt>, <v Speaker>, <lang>, plus inline timestamps. STYLE blocks (CSS) must come before the first cue.
EncodingNot fixed by any standard. UTF-8 is the safe choice; older files may use other encodings.Must be UTF-8.
Cue order and overlapListed in time order by convention.Cues must be listed by start time. Overlapping cues are allowed.
Separator between cuesA blank line.A blank line.

The same two cues in each format

Converting SRT to VTT by hand: add the WEBVTT line and a blank line at the top, change every comma in the timestamps to a period, and keep or drop the cue numbers (in VTT they become optional identifiers).

Common mistakes that break files

Wrong timestamp separator
A comma in a VTT timestamp is invalid, and players that follow the W3C parsing rules drop that cue. A period in an SRT timestamp is accepted by some players and rejected by others. Use a comma in SRT and a period in VTT.
Missing blank line between cues
Without the blank line, the next cue number and timing are read as text of the cue above, so one cue shows the wrong text and the next one disappears.
Overlapping cues
WebVTT allows overlaps, and players then show both cues together. In SRT, players differ. Accidental overlaps usually come from shifting timings or merging files; make sure each cue ends before the next one starts unless two lines really should appear at once.
End time not after start time
A zero-length or negative cue never shows. It usually comes from a typo or a bad time shift.
BOM and encoding
Text saved in a legacy encoding and opened as UTF-8 shows garbled accented letters (for example Ã© instead of é). WebVTT must be UTF-8 and may start with a byte order mark. In SRT a byte order mark can show up as stray characters before the first number in some tools, so UTF-8 without BOM is safest.
Empty cues
A cue with timing but no text puts nothing on screen and can confuse editors and converters. Delete it or add the missing line.
A bad header in VTT
The first line must be WEBVTT. Text such as WEBVTT FILE works only because a space follows the word; WEBVTTFILE or a blank first line makes the whole file invalid.
Lines that are too long or too fast
Technically valid, but hard to read. Use the calculator and checker below.

When to use which

  • Use WebVTT for subtitles and captions on web pages: browsers support it natively through the HTML <track> element, and it supports positioning, speaker voices and styling.
  • Use SRT when a platform, editor or client asks for it. It is simple and widely accepted by video editors, desktop players and upload forms.
  • Check the brief first. Festivals, broadcasters and streaming services often require their own delivery formats. The BBC, for example, asks for EBU-TT and EBU-TT-D rather than SRT or VTT.

Reading speed calculator

Reading speed is measured as characters per second (cps) or words per minute (wpm). Published guidelines use different measures and figures:

Published reading speed figures
SourceWhat it states
Netflix, English (USA) Timed Text Style GuideAdult programs: up to 20 characters per second. Children's programs: up to 17 characters per second. 42 characters per line.
BBC Subtitle GuidelinesRecommended subtitle speed 160-180 words per minute (0.33 to 0.375 seconds per word). The BBC adds that viewers tend to prefer verbatim subtitles, so the rate may be adjusted to the pace of the programme.
DCMP Captioning Key, Presentation RateFor educational media: lower level not to exceed 130 wpm, middle level 140 wpm, upper level 160 wpm. Speaker IDs and sound effects count as words.

How this page counts: characters include letters, spaces and punctuation, with tags removed and line breaks not counted. Words are runs of text separated by spaces. Other tools may count slightly differently.

Client transcript style checklist

Transcripts delivered to a client are usually judged against that client's style guide, and guides disagree on the details. Settle each of these before you start, and write the answer down.

  1. Verbatim level

    Full verbatim keeps everything said: fillers (um, uh), false starts, repetitions and stutters. Clean verbatim removes those without changing meaning or the speaker's wording. Ask which one the client wants, and whether some items are always kept (for example, laughter or meaningful hesitation).

  2. Speaker labels

    Pick one format and use it for every turn: full name, first name, role (INTERVIEWER, RESPONDENT) or Speaker 1 / Speaker 2. Check capitalisation, bold, and the colon after the label. Never use two labels for the same person.

  3. Tags and notation

    Use the client's exact wording and brackets for unclear audio, crosstalk, background sounds and foreign-language speech, for example [inaudible 00:12:31] or [crosstalk]. Do not invent new tags. Ask whether an unclear word should be guessed with a marker or tagged.

  4. Timestamps

    Confirm the format ([HH:MM:SS] or [MM:SS]), where it goes (every speaker change, every paragraph, or at a fixed interval), and whether tags like inaudible carry their own time.

  5. Numbers

    Number rules vary even between published guides. The Netflix English (USA) guide writes out zero to ten and uses numerals above ten; the BBC guidelines say one to ten are often better spelled out but use numerals with units. Both spell out a number that starts a sentence. Ask how the client handles money, percentages, dates and times.

  6. Spelling and punctuation

    US or UK spelling, how to mark interruptions and trailing speech, and whether profanity is kept or masked.

  7. Final pass

    Search the finished file for each tag and label to catch inconsistent spellings, and check the export format the client asked for (DOCX, TXT, PDF, SRT or VTT).
